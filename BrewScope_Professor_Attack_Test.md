# BrewScope — Professor Attack Test

## 1. Why ₹79?
The directional target-market input places the comfortable range around ₹50–70 and ₹79 as the upper acceptable boundary. 200 ml at ₹79 = ₹39.50/100 ml. The final concept should be stress-tested against actual unit economics.

## 2. Why Sleepy Owl?
It already participates in RTD coffee and has a specialty coffee positioning, making this a plausible adjacency.

## 3. Why not Nescafé / BRU / Cavin's?
They compete effectively on mass price and familiarity. BrewScope is not claiming a taste advantage without testing; it proposes a functional occasion and job-to-be-done.

## 4. Why not NERV?
Because NERV proves that generic focus RTD is not empty. The project deliberately narrows the whitespace to accessible price + coffee-first + late-night study/work.

## 5. What proves the gap?
The answer is a triangulation of:
- observed Indian shelf structure
- functional-claim density
- directional consumer input
- brand fit
A gap remains a hypothesis until concept and economic testing.

## 6. What did AI contribute?
AI helped generate hypotheses and competitor structures. The team overrode multiple AI recommendations after evidence review. This is central to the assignment's stated objective of reconciling coursework with AI output.

## 7. Why no primary survey?
The faculty instructions for Topic 3 require a functional app and genuine underlying analysis; they do not explicitly mandate a survey. Our consumer input is therefore used qualitatively and transparently, not presented as statistically representative evidence.

## 8. What is the managerial recommendation?
Proceed to concept development and controlled testing — not full launch approval.
